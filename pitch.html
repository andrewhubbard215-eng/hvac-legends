HVAC Allstars

Laboratory practice on the phone students already carry, and on the computer in the classroom.

Professor Andrew Hubbard · A program briefing

01 · The problem

Access ends when class ends.

  • Current trainers require a desktop, a login, and a scheduled laboratory period.
  • Students will not open that software on a phone.
  • The hours between classes go unused. That is where additional practice belongs.

Allstars is that phone laboratory. The full lab still runs on the classroom computer.

02 · The laboratories

Three labs. One program.

Sized for a phone. Available in the classroom while class is in session.

  • System sandbox. A live refrigeration cycle. Faults behave as they do in the field.
  • Recovery. Recover, pressure-test with nitrogen, evacuate, weigh the charge, and leak-check.
  • Section 608. Core and Types I, II, and III, then a practice exam that explains each answer.

03 · A week of practice

Work they can repeat.

  • System sandboxSuperheat, subcooling, pressure, and temperature respond to the change. High head pressure shuts the compressor off.
  • RecoveryFive stations: a DOT cylinder, a nitrogen test, vacuum decay, the ounces on the data tag, and the flare.
  • ElectricalA 24-volt ladder, then the meter on the component. The open is the first dead reading after a live one.
  • Service callsA customer, the readings, and a diagnosis. Refrigerant handling is part of the grade.
  • The cycleDischarge stays on the high side. The refrigerant flashes on the low side.

04 · Instruction

Procedure, then the drill.

How-to videos with Dave LoBuono sit on the laboratory. Students hear the procedure, then perform it. They do not leave the program to find a video.

  • The podcast is the same lesson when they are not looking at a screen.
  • The drill is the assignment. The video and the podcast are a second hearing.
  • Shop iConnect trainers are virtual units. The card lists the steps and plays the video.

05 · EPA Section 608

Study, then the practice exam.

Core, Type I, Type II, and Type III. Students work the vacuum chart until they can use it. At 80 percent on the lessons, the practice exam opens. Each item explains the correct answer and the wrong ones.

Practice only. The certification card is issued by an EPA-approved proctor.

Student labs. Instructor desk.

  • HVAC Legends is the student laboratory. It does not include this desk.

Begin with one class. The same labs are already on their phones.

08 · Licensing

One seat. One student. One term.

Student seat · $499

The full program. About 1,200 hours across five terms. A named seat, on the student's phone and on the lab computer. The desk is not included.

Or $199 for one term. Still one named seat, not a login the class shares.

Instructor seat

One per class. The desk, the class day, additional labs, and the materials list at the end of the module.

  • The school leases the seats. Students do not buy the application.
  • One person per seat. Add a seat when the roster grows. Release it when the student leaves.
  • Begin with one instructor seat and one class.

Speaker note. $499 sits between a textbook and a tool kit. Program tuition is about $30,630. In the room, a term may be offered at $149. Do not print a lower figure. Above $249 a term, or $699 for the program, the school compares it with the $150 technology fee and declines.

09 · Outcomes

A procedure they can repeat.

  • Begin with the complaint. Check airflow, then electrical, then refrigerant.
  • Read superheat and subcooling together. One number is not a diagnosis.
  • Do not replace a safety until you know what it monitors. A pressure switch: compare the cut-out to the rating. A float switch: check the pan, the trap, and the vent.
  • Recover into the correct cylinder and stop the tank at 80 percent. The Section 608 card authorizes the purchase of refrigerant.
